Three ways to build it like somebody meant it.

Same product, same features, same data. What changes is the thinking underneath — a real type scale, a real grid, purposeful colour, and a home screen that answers a question instead of decorating one. Every screen below uses real content from the live app.

First, why the current build reads as generated

  • 4 typefacesPublic Sans, Bricolage Grotesque and Spline Sans Mono are all loaded — and Times is rendering somewhere, which means at least one element has no font assigned at all. Designed systems use two, maybe three, on purpose.
  • 9 type sizes, no scale8, 9, 12, 14, 15, 16, 20, 40, 62px. There is no ratio between them, 14 and 15 do two versions of one job, and 8px and 9px are below the readable minimum on a phone.
  • 4 corner radii10px, 16px, 38px and 50% all in one screen. Radius is a brand signature; four of them is four brands.
  • Emoji as iconography🤖 and 🔒 are doing the work of an icon set. They render differently on every device and cannot be coloured, sized or aligned.
  • A decorative homeThe orbiting circle hub is the first thing a client sees. It shows six destinations and zero information — not where they stand, not what is blocking them, not what happens next. It also cannot grow: a seventh destination breaks the ring.
  • Empty states that end"Your secure document store is empty" describes a condition and offers no way out of it. Every empty state is a chance to start the thing it is empty of.
Direction A

The Ledger

Keeps the warm paper feel the app already has, and gives it discipline. One serif-free type scale, hairline rules instead of floating cards, tabular numerals, and a single green that only ever means "verified". It reads like a private bank's statement — calm, exact, and nothing on screen that is not load-bearing.
